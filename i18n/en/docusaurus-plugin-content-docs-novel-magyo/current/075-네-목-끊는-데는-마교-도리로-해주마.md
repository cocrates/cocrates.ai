# Chapter 075: 네 목 끊는 데는 마교 도리로 해주마

## Episode 001: 빈칸을 채우는 절차

The challenge file came back from the document room with more blank spaces than writing.

Acceptance. Terms. Venue. Result. Disposition.

The clerk placed it on the table between Taeui and Gojin. The paper was rough, and the ink at its edge had dried in a crooked crescent. Gojin tapped his elder token with one finger. The sound was small, but he made it carry.

“The challenge was received yesterday,” the clerk said. “Receipt is not acceptance. This office will now record the competent acceptance and the conditions.”

Taeui glanced at the witnesses gathered behind the clerk. Jeomchang men stood in a tight line, blue sleeves brushing. The minor sects had not formed a line at all. They stood in uneven knots, as if each man had come from a different road and refused to be arranged by somebody else.

Cheongun stood among them with his torn right sleeve and the wrap around his wrist. His thin sword remained at his side. He had not recovered. He had only learned how to stand while hurting.

Gojin raised his chin.

“A one-on-one public duel. No interference. The loser acknowledges the result before the witnesses.”

“And the petition?”

Taeui pointed at the separate bundle tied with red thread.

The clerk answered before Gojin could.

“It remains in investigation custody. The duel result will not, by itself, decide guilt, responsibility, ownership, or punishment. Those fields belong to their own review.”

Gojin’s finger stopped on the token.

“Then what is being tested?”

“Your mouth,” Taeui said. “It made the challenge.”

Several minor-sect men lowered their heads to hide their smiles. Gojin’s beard twitched.

“My qualification is being tested. You called yourself a direct investigator while standing over a senior of a great sect.”

“I called a missing record missing. If your qualification is hidden inside your wrist, take it out.”

The clerk looked from one to the other. “The challenger is Gojin, third elder of Jeomchang. The challenged party is Namgung Taeui, acting in his personal capacity. Venue: the Alliance practice yard. Witnesses: this office, the named sect representatives, and the present small-sect delegates. No outside fighter may enter the ring. A surrender or inability to continue ends the duel.”

“Accepted,” Gojin said.

Taeui leaned back.

“I accept the duel. I do not withdraw the evidence.”

The clerk’s brush paused.

“Record that sentence separately,” Taeui said. “Otherwise someone will later pretend a sword erased a date.”

The brush moved. The blank spaces narrowed.

Cheongun stepped closer, careful not to put weight on his wrapped side.

“Will the witness bundle remain with the clerk?”

“Until the result is entered,” the clerk said.

Cheongun nodded. He did not ask whether Taeui would win. He checked where the paper would be kept.

Taeui looked at him once.

“If you stare at the documents during a duel, I’ll charge you for making the audience nervous.”

“I am watching the witnesses.”

“That is worse.”

The clerk pressed the Alliance mark onto the challenge sheet. It was not the red seal that made a final ruling. It was a smaller receipt mark, enough to show that the procedure had moved to its next line.

The sheet was carried to the practice yard.

Gojin followed with his hand on his sword. His face had the stiff calm of a man who believed that a proper form could still repair a broken argument.

Taeui walked behind him, bored enough to yawn.

## Episode 002: 네 번의 소리

The practice yard was a square of packed sand enclosed by low rails. The wind had pushed dust into the corners. The clerk stood outside the ring with the challenge sheet. Cheongun stood beside him, not inside the witness line and not far enough away to miss the feet.

Gojin drew his sword.

The first line went for Taeui’s throat.

Taeui moved half a step.

The blade passed where his neck had been. Two fingers touched Gojin’s wrist. The sword point swung toward empty air, and a thin spray of sand rose under its tip.

“First form,” Taeui said. “You announced it before you used it.”

Gojin twisted and cut for the waist. Taeui bent backward just enough for the steel to skim his robe. He did not retreat. His hand touched Gojin’s elbow, and the elder’s shoulder turned a fraction too far. The sword struck the ground.

“Second.”

“Do not mock the Pyeongcheon sequence!”

“Then stop showing it to me.”

Gojin’s sword rose again. His forms were clean. That was the problem. Clean lines were easy to read when the person using them had been praised for keeping them clean. He cut high, changed angle, and drove forward with a thrust that would have opened an ordinary opponent from collar to ribs.

Taeui entered instead of leaving.

The sword passed behind his shoulder. His palm met the flat of the blade and sent it aside. His other hand pressed the wrist. Gojin’s front foot slid through the sand.

The minor-sect delegates shifted as one. They had come to see whether the records could survive a great sect’s sword. Now they saw the great sect’s sword unable to touch the person holding the records.

Gojin pulled free and made three cuts without pause.

Taeui avoided the first by turning his hip. He caught the second at the wrist. The third stopped because the elbow had already lost its path.

“You are not fighting,” Gojin shouted.

“I am. You are presenting.”

The elder’s face reddened. His sword made a hard circle that drove dust against the rails. He pushed his inner strength through the hilt. The ring seemed to tighten around him.

Taeui watched the feet.

The right heel lifted first.

Gojin came in close, abandoning the named forms. His shoulder struck forward. His sword hand turned for a short cut. There was no room left between them for a graceful sequence.

There was, however, room for four mistakes.

Taeui’s finger struck the outside of Gojin’s elbow.

The sound was dry.

The sword fell from Gojin’s hand.

“You—!”

Gojin swung with the other arm. Taeui caught the wrist and turned it against the direction of the shoulder.

The second sound cracked through the yard.

Gojin’s knees buckled. Taeui’s foot touched behind one knee, not hard, only at the point where balance became a decision.

The elder dropped to one knee.

“This is not a lawful—”

“You requested a result.”

Gojin tried to rise. Taeui’s heel met the side of his knee.

The third sound came lower.

Cheongun did not blink. His fingers tightened around his scabbard. The old instinct in him wanted to step forward when a body fell. He stayed where the witness line placed him. The clerk had not called for aid. The duel had not ended.

Gojin’s face twisted. He reached for the dropped sword.

Taeui took the ankle.

The fourth sound was quiet compared with the others. It still made every man outside the rail understand that Gojin would not stand again today.

Taeui released him and stepped back. He did not put a blade to the elder’s throat.

Gojin lay on the sand, both arms and both legs useless for the moment. His sword was beyond his fingers.

“Surrender,” the clerk called.

“The procedure cannot—”

“Surrender,” the clerk repeated. “Or inability to continue.”

Gojin’s hand scraped the sand. The token at his belt had stopped tapping.

“I acknowledge the result,” he said.

The clerk wrote it down.

He recorded the time, the witnesses, the absence of outside intervention, the surrender, and the physical result. Then he folded the challenge sheet and opened a second form.

“The correction form is separate,” he said. “It is not a guilt judgment. It records the condition your challenge was made to secure.”

Gojin’s eyes moved to Taeui.

“You planned this.”

“No. You did. I only read the part where your mouth moved.”

The clerk read the limited wording aloud. The current Qingfeng accusation lacked sufficient basis on the records presently held. The seized material was to be returned through custody. Access to the medicinal field was to be restored pending separate review. Ownership, the upper chain, and responsibility for any other record remained open.

Gojin’s breathing became rough.

“You want me to sign that while I am on the ground?”

“You wanted an honest result,” Taeui said. “The ground is honest.”

Gojin tried to raise his right hand. It trembled and fell.

The clerk placed a brush near his fingers. Taeui did not help him hold it. The clerk did. That was the clerk’s custody, not Taeui’s authority.

Gojin pressed the brush to the paper.

His signature came out crooked. The ink spread at the last character.

The clerk took the form away before anyone could touch it.

“Duel result and correction received,” he announced. “Further responsibility review remains separate.”

The minor sects did not cheer at once. They looked at the two papers, one naming a defeat and one refusing to name more than it could prove.

Then someone at the back exhaled.

The sound traveled farther than a shout.

## Episode 003: 되찾은 문패

Two days later, the correction form arrived at Qingfeng Gate.

The blue sign still had a broken corner. The outer wall had not repaired itself. The training yard still showed the split post from the earlier attack. The gate looked exactly like a place that had survived, not a place that had been magically restored.

Cheongun read the first line.

The current accusation against Qingfeng lacked sufficient basis in the records presently held.

He read it again.

Below it were the practical orders. Seized material would be returned through the recorded custody route. Access to the medicinal field would be restored pending separate review. No sentence declared ownership. No sentence named the hand behind the false record. No sentence promised that another accusation could never arrive.

Cheongun let out a breath he had been keeping since the gate was first surrounded.

“It is not everything,” he said.

“It is what the paper can carry.”

Taeui stood beneath the sign with his hands in his sleeves. His face showed no pride. It showed the same boredom he wore before breaking four joints.

The first small sect arrived with two sacks of grain. Another brought wrapped medicine. A third brought a written statement supporting open record access and witness copies. None of them brought a banner. None asked Qingfeng to kneel.

One of the delegates bowed to Cheongun.

“This is support for the gate and the people inside it. Not payment to the investigator.”

“We know,” Cheongun said.

The delegate looked at Taeui anyway.

“And for him?”

“He already took his payment,” Taeui said. “Gojin’s bribe was expensive.”

The gathered men laughed. Taeui’s mouth tightened as if laughter were a draft coming under a door.

Cheongun turned to him.

“You did not have to accept the duel.”

“I did not accept it for you.”

“I did not say that.”

“Your face did.”

Cheongun folded the correction carefully. His wrist wrap pulled when he bent it. The pain crossed his expression and disappeared. He did not call for a healer. He did not change the sword at his side. He simply placed the paper beneath the cracked sign.

At that moment, a messenger arrived from the Alliance.

The second sealed paper was smaller than the correction. Its red mark was clean, without the dust that had gathered on the first document.

Taeui opened it.

The invitation named a martial tournament. It gave a date and an arrival requirement. It did not list the participants. It did not explain the contests. It did not mention any medicine, bloodline, or hidden scheme.

“Are you going?” Cheongun asked.

“That is why they sent it.”

“May I come?”

Taeui looked at the thin sword, the repaired belt, and the wrapped wrist. Cheongun had not recovered. He was still standing because he had decided standing was necessary.

“Keep up.”

Cheongun bowed.

“Do not bow. It makes the gate look taller than it is.”

The correction form lay beneath the blue sign. The tournament invitation lay on top of it. Wind lifted both papers together, then let them fall against the cracked wood.

Behind them, small-sect men unloaded grain and medicine. Qingfeng’s door remained open.

Taeui looked at the new seal instead of the old crowd.

“A tournament,” he said. “At least the next fools will have better uniforms.”
