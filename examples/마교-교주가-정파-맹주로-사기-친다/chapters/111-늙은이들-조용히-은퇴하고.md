## Purpose
To turn Chapter 110's public conditions into an enforceable, source-separated responsibility table without converting procedural execution into a guilt verdict.

## Reader Engagement
### Personal Stake
If the table is blurred, the elders can turn a bounded judgment into either scapegoating or escape; Taeui must make the record usable without owning the judgment.
### Opening Question
Can a public responsibility table remain honest when every party wants its blank fields filled by someone else?
### Reader Questions
- Who is responsible for submitting each missing source?
- Can the orthodox schools pressure the process without manufacturing a verdict?
- What is Taeui allowed to do, and what must Jin retain?
- Which row will be questioned first in 112?
### Stakes
The credibility of the post-hearing process and the practical balance between public pressure and final Alliance authority.

## Chapter Arc
Taeui lays out 110's conditions as a table. Jin approves its publication and fixes the boundary between execution and judgment. Hye-gyeong tries to fold the Buddhist proposal into the table, while Myeongjin tries to erase his row by disputing the table's language. Taeui separates both attempts into non-dispositive notes. Major-school representatives publicly demand dates and receipts, forcing the table to become operational. The chapter closes when Jin assigns the first responsibility-confirmation question for 112, while the answer remains blank.
