## Reader Engagement (Chapter Level)
### Personal Stake
Taeui's public legitimacy and control over the source-separated records can be challenged by a document designed to make refusal look guilty.
### Opening Question
Who turned the monument's public memory into a nine-school legal weapon?
### Reader Questions
What charges can a formal summons hide? Will Taeui enter the elders' frame or make them enter his?
### Stakes
Refusal lets the elders claim evasion; acceptance gives them a stage but leaves Taeui able to bring only the records he chooses.

## Chapter Arc
The completed monument exposes the gap between public memory and elder authority. Taeui checks the original ledger custody instead of chasing the messengers, then lets the nine-school coalition convert its unopened demand into a formal summons. At Chwiungak he accepts attendance without accepting their framing, and the chapter closes on the summons being fixed as 102's first question.
