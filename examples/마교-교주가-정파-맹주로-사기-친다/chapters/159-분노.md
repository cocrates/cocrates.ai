## Purpose
To show anger being contained by a precise intake record rather than converted into unauthorized access or judgment.

## Reader Engagement (Chapter Level)
### Personal Stake
The danger is not only the request's charge but the temptation to let anger fill its blank fields.
### Opening Question
When a request arrives already carrying an accusation, who has the authority to decide what it is allowed to become?
### Reader Questions
- What can be recorded without opening the requested material?
- Can a procedural seal restrain anger without becoming a verdict?
- What will the separately numbered next request ask for?
### Stakes
If the team confuses intake with authorization, the hold protecting the unresolved case collapses. If they preserve the boundary, the unanswered request remains usable without becoming evidence.

## Chapter Arc
The request is presented with a charged requester-provided title. Taeui strips the title from the finding and creates a separate intake row. Jin rejects access and execution, then seals only the procedural state. Cheongun confirms that no approved physical/custody scope exists and therefore gives no physical report. Taeui records the three distinct statuses and leaves substantive fields blank. Before the session closes, he numbers a separate 160 request draft, preserving the same boundary.
