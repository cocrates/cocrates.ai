## Purpose
To process the unapproved custody-scope request as a bounded procedural dispute without converting scope, authorization, or custody into responsibility or adjudication.

## Reader Engagement (Chapter Level)
### Personal Stake
Taeui must prevent an empty administrative request from becoming a conclusion, while Jin must protect the authority boundary without pretending that procedure itself resolves the underlying gap.
### Opening Question
When a request names a custody scope but leaves every authority field blank, what exactly is being asked for?
### Reader Questions
Which fields can be opened, who can authorize them, and what remains impossible to claim even after a lawful access decision?
### Stakes
An overbroad entry could silently become an accusation; an over-narrow entry could erase the procedural dispute. The record's blank fields must survive intact.

## Chapter Arc
Taeui receives the unapproved request and decomposes its ambiguous language. Jin hears the dispute and authorizes only a named procedural-field access scope, explicitly refusing execution, authentication, comparison, and adjudication. Cheongun records observable custody and condition within that scope; Taeui then seals a record that preserves blank results and responsibility fields and opens a separate, unapproved Chapter 146 review docket.
