## Required Persona Checks
#### Target Reader
- Stance: Values fast web-serial pressure, mockery of hypocrisy, and a concrete next-chapter pull.
- Strengths: Opens with a physical blockade, turns identical protests into immediate curiosity, and ends on Jin's direct voice.
- Defects: The procedural distinctions could slow a reader if presented as explanation rather than conflict; the manuscript keeps them inside hostile exchanges and document handling. Severity Low.
- Reader impact: The reader gets a partial win without losing the larger threat, which supports continuation.

#### Genre Critic
- Stance: Checks the saïda martial-arts promise and whether orthodox hypocrisy is exposed without flattening the conflict.
- Strengths: Taeui's questions puncture the representatives' slogans, while the complaint remains a live institutional weapon.
- Defects: No physical retaliation occurs in this chapter. Severity Low; the chapter's promised pressure is procedural and hands directly to Jin.
- Reader impact: The payoff is a controlled humiliation and escalation rather than a final beatdown.

#### Reader-Editor
- Stance: Checks mobile pacing, scene handoffs, exposition restraint, and serialization value.
- Strengths: Three clean movements, recurring red-seal image, and a final spoken hook.
- Defects: Several record comparisons repeat the same pressure pattern. Severity Low; repetition reinforces the empty-record motif and is interrupted by Gyeong's boundary.
- Reader impact: The unresolved protest provides a reason to continue instead of closing the case too early.

#### Literary Critic
- Stance: Checks rhythm, motif, sensory detail, voice, and emotional restraint.
- Strengths: Red seal, empty sack, and blank responsibility field carry authority versus material absence; Taeui's outer politeness contrasts with his testing gaze.
- Defects: Some procedural exposition is direct. Severity Low; it remains character-led and avoids a closing theme statement.
- Reader impact: The institutional scene retains texture and forward tension.
