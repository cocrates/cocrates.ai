## Design Critique

#### Target Reader
- Stance: Wants the emotional cost after the judgment, but also expects the series row’s concrete succession move.
- Strengths: Immediate poison crisis, one-month limit, and unvarnished father-son confrontation are compelling.
- Defects: The handover is softened from “entrusts the sect” to “asks whether Jinwoo can take it” → High → make the entrustment an observable act while keeping the final path as a question.
- Reader impact: The concrete succession payoff must land before the 120 curiosity question.

#### Genre Critic
- Stance: Tests revenge-aftercare against the male-oriented serial contract.
- Strengths: The dose is not a reset, the father remains guilty, and the one-month clock creates urgency.
- Defects: Same Hook-strength mismatch → High → execute the handover, do not only negotiate it.
- Reader impact: A concrete transfer preserves progression instead of making the episode feel like a pause.

#### Plot Expert
- Stance: Tests causality, Hook alignment, and scope.
- Strengths: 118 relapse causally drives the dose; TH-174/145 advance and TH-175 is held.
- Defects: Series Hook/body mismatch in Scene 4 → High → revise Summary/Scene 4 to say Dohyun entrusts the sect to Jinwoo; leave only the shared-path answer open.
- Reader impact: Prevents a false cliffhanger on an obligation scheduled for this episode.

#### Reader-Editor
- Stance: Tests serialized unit completion and click-through.
- Strengths: Dose → time limit → responsibility → succession/path is a sellable sequence.
- Defects: Same concrete-handover gap → Med → fix in ④; do not add exposition.
- Reader impact: The episode needs a completed beat before its final question.

#### Character Critic
- Stance: Tests profile-backed motive and relationship pressure.
- Strengths: Dohyun refuses both self-exoneration and forced reconciliation; Jinwoo separates treatment from forgiveness.
